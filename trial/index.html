Before you import clients

Choose one real workflow to replay with dummy data. For example, a client books three visits, changes one time, adds a medication note, receives a post-visit update and pays an invoice. Repeat it in each platform. A demo full of idealized sample clients does not reveal what happens when an owner changes a plan after you have assigned a sitter.

Use a temporary client record rather than entering real addresses, door codes or medical notes into three trial accounts. Test access controls with invented details. Once you pick a platform, decide what to import and tell clients how you will use their data.

A two-week test

1
Days 1–2: map the current workWrite down where booking requests, confirmations, key details, visit notes and invoices live today. Count the handoffs, not just the clicks.
2
Days 3–5: simulate the bookingEnter a multi-visit request, holiday rate, pet-specific care note and a last-minute change. Confirm what the client sees.
3
Days 6–8: test executionAssign a second sitter, change the assignment, record check-in and send a photo update. Verify which notes each role can read.
4
Days 9–11: test moneyCreate and correct an invoice; check processor fees, payment timing and what happens on a refund.
5
Days 12–14: test exit and costFind exports, cancellation controls and help response time. Price a month with your actual team size.

Decision rule

Keep a platform only if it reliably removes a failure point. If a $25 plan saves an hour per month but still makes you copy client instructions manually, the number of features is not the deciding factor. If a $50 plan prevents a missed visit or an invoice error, a higher price may be justified. Record the outcome you observed, not a general claim about the product.

If none works, keep a simple process for now and revisit after your visit volume or staff count changes. A trial is not a promise to subscribe.
